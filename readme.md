🚀 Supply Chain Service Reliability & Customer Fulfillment OverhaulThis project documents the root cause analysis and action plan developed to address a critical collapse in a major company's customer service and order fulfillment metrics.The Business Problem: Total Service Failure (OTIF %) plummeted to $\mathbf{29.02\%}$—a $\mathbf{36\%}$ deviation from the target—threatening high-risk customer churn.The Key Finding: Advanced diagnostic metrics proved the failure was structural: $\mathbf{96.59\%}$ Warehouse Capacity (VOFR) was sufficient, but item-level stockout frequency (LIFR) was failing at $\mathbf{65.96\%}$. Business Problem and ObjectiveThe objective was to provide an immediate course correction for a company experiencing severe customer churn risk and operational inefficiency due to a service collapse.Core Objectives:Root Cause Diagnosis: Define the precise point of failure (Inventory vs. Logistics).Risk Quantification: Identify the Top 5 High-Risk Customers and the most affected Geographical Region.Action Plan Development: Create a quantified, two-part strategy targeting the Top 5 SKUs and the largest logistical failure bucket ($\mathbf{8.3K}$ 1-Day delays).<a id="2-data-sources-and-etl-process"></a>2. Data Sources and ETL Process2.1 Raw Data SourcesAll source files are located in the C2 Input for participants/ folder and include transactional, dimensional, and target data.File NameDescriptionfact_order_lines.csvKey Source: Transaction-level order details (fulfillment, delay).dim_customers.csvCustomer demographics and geography.dim_products.csvProduct hierarchy and codes.metrics_list.xlsxBusiness targets and KPI definitions.2.2 Transformation MethodologyThe primary analytical contribution was the creation of diagnostic metrics in SQL to decouple inventory control from bulk capacity:Line-Item Fill Rate (LIFR %): Measures SKU-level stockout frequency. Low LIFR = Inventory Control Problem.Volume Fill Rate (VOFR %): Measures if the warehouse had enough total capacity. High VOFR = Capacity is sufficient.The ETL logic is defined entirely within the supply_chain.sql script.<a id="3-dashboard-1-executive-insights"></a>3.
